Tolstoy wrote *The Power of Darkness* (Власть тьмы) in two or three weeks in the autumn of 1886, bedridden after a leg injury, for the popular stage. Its plot is almost a transcript: a real Tula criminal case — a peasant who fathered a child on his stepdaughter, killed the newborn, and confessed publicly at her wedding — given to him by his friend the prosecutor N. V. Davydov, who arranged for him to meet the convicted man twice. The one thing Tolstoy invented was the poisoning of the husband, which turns the wife into the engine of the tragedy.

The play is built on a proverb, which it kept as its subtitle: *a claw caught, the whole bird is lost.* One unrepented sin pulls a whole life into the dark. The father, Akim — the least articulate man on stage, all stammer and "tae" — warns his son of it at the start; the son names it back, fulfilled, at the end, kneeling to confess every crime before the assembled wedding. The redemption the play offers is not in the horror that made it famous (the infanticide, which Tolstoy staged almost entirely through speech and then rewrote a gentler version of). It is in the confession, and in the father's rapture: "You did not spare yourself — He will spare you."

The dive set out to test whether the play is "art for the people" practised a decade before *What Is Art?* theorised it. On the doctrine, yes; on the audience, the primary record complicates it sharply. The forty Yasnaya Polyana peasants who first heard it read aloud did not understand it — and Tolstoy's friends then *used* that failure to argue the play past the censor as "above the masses." It triumphed instead in aristocratic salons, at the imperial court, and among students. Alexander III praised it ("a wonderful thing") and ordered it staged; Pobedonostsev, calling it cruder than Zola, reversed him in a day, and the stage ban held for eight years while the printed text sold over 100,000 cheap copies and the play premièred in Paris. By January 1887 Tolstoy was describing it, in a letter, as written "for the 'great world'" — not the people after all. He came, by March, to call it "my unhappy drama."
