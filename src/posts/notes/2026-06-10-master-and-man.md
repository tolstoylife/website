Tolstoy conceived *Master and Man* (*Хозяин и работник*) in bed on the morning of 6 September 1894 — "a very vivid artistic story about a master and a workman" — drafted it in a week, dismissed it as "very uninteresting," and put it away for three months. A merchant and his servant lose their way in a blizzard; the merchant, Brekhunov, dies covering the peasant Nikita with his own body, and dying feels that "he is Nikita and Nikita is he."

The [dive](../../../docs/research/1894-1895-master-and-man/index.html) reads the novella against its own composition record, and the manuscript record holds a surprise. The death scene everyone remembers — the master saving the man, the two selves merging, "Nikita is alive, so I too am alive" — was not the seed. In the first draft Brekhunov's awakening is solitary and self-regarding, and the story ends with Nikita simply rolling the frozen master off "like a log" and walking to a village alone. The substitutionary death was built across later redactions and reached its final form only at the *Severny Vestnik* galley-proof stage, in February 1895. The diary agrees to the day: on 6 January, after reading the story aloud at a neighbour's estate, Tolstoy wrote "now I know what to do."

That turns the dive's three claims from interpretation into something the manuscripts can be made to show: that the story gives narrative flesh to the doctrine of [*On Life*](../../../docs/research/1886-1887-on-life/index.html) (true life is the renunciation of the separate self for love); that it routes salvation through the *exploiter* — the master who underpays his man and thinks himself generous — rather than the meek peasant who is already at peace; and that it is a lived specimen of the "universal religious art" Tolstoy would define two years later in [*What Is Art?*](../../../docs/research/1897-1898-what-is-art/index.html).

It was an instant phenomenon — 15,000 copies in four days, uncensored — and Tolstoy disliked it to the end ("I hear praise from all sides, but I do not like it"). The writing ran straight through the worst weeks of his marriage and the death of his youngest son, Vanechka, on 23 February 1895, as the proofs went out. A parable of dying well, finished in the house of a child's dying.
