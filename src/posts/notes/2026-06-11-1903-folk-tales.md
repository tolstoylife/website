New corpus dive: [The народные рассказы of 1903](https://github.com/tolstoylife/tolstoy.life/tree/main/docs/research/1903-folk-tales) — a theme-dive across the five folk tales Tolstoy wrote in 1903, sixteen years after the Posrednik cycle: «Ассирийский царь Асархадон», «Труд, смерть и болезнь», «Три вопроса», «Это ты», and the satirical legend «Разрушение ада и восстановление его».

The genesis is the Kishinev pogrom of April 1903. Tolstoy signed a public protest, told Sholom Aleichem the real culprit was «одно правительство» — and that he could not say so «в русском легальном издании». What he could not print as statement he published as legend: three tales, written in three weeks of midsummer («Написал три сказки. Еще плохо, но может быть порядочно»), sent out in Yiddish for the relief almanac «Гилф».

One doctrine runs through all five — the unity of all life, «это ты» / tat tvam asi. Asarhadon is made to live his victim's death and hear that «жизнь одна во всем»; the tyrant of «Это ты» learns that «преступник это — ты». Two of the tales are twins from a single German theosophical source («Das bist du», *Theosophischer Wegweiser* 1903) — one Tolstoy's near-translation, one his original elaboration. The fifth, «Разрушение ада», is the doctrine's inverse: Beelzebub's devils explaining how the Church, the state, war and science rebuilt hell after Christ destroyed it. Conceived as an illustration to «Обращение к духовенству» and rooted in an 1879 legend from the same skazitel', Shchegolyonok, who fed the 1880s tales, it was banned in Russia in 1906, 1910 and 1913 and printed there only in 1917.

The standing reading classifies the trio as occasioned "aid literature." The primary record shows something else — the народный рассказ as the encrypted form of a forbidden political truth, carrying Tolstoy's most compressed late metaphysics, given to the relief fund under the 1891 copyright renunciation as «право первого печатания».

The dossier carries five work-record proposals (genre `parable`), a 12-entity routing map for later wiki ingestion (Sholom Aleichem and the Kishinev pogrom first), and a recommendation to close a dangling genre-enum item left by the parent dive. All 29 quotes pass the byte-fidelity check.
