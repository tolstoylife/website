Tolstoy told his closest collaborator exactly what *Father Sergius* was about, and it was not what most readers take from it. Writing to Vladimir Chertkov on 16 February 1891, he called the novella's famous carnal struggle "одна ступень" — one rung — and named the real subject "славой людской," human glory, the craving for other people's esteem. The story of a monk who cuts off his own finger to resist a seductress is, by its author's account, a story about the vanity hidden inside the wish to be a saint.

This dive reads the novella against its own composition record: the eight-year, four-redaction genesis; the February 1890 visit to Optina Pustyn that fed its hostile portrait of monastic life; the diary note in which Tolstoy grounds the hero's pride in two real men he knew; and the decision, in July 1898, to finish the work and print it — with *Resurrection* — to raise money for the Doukhobors' emigration to Canada. It was never published while he lived.

A few things the primary record makes plain:

- **The cure the work proposes is not a better asceticism but the abandonment of holiness as a project** — the obscure, self-forgetful service of the cousin Pashenka, "who lives for God, imagining that she lives for people." Tolstoy stated the logic in a single sentence to V. P. Zolotarev: "Надо опустить руки, чтобы стать на ноги" — one must let go to stand on one's feet.
- **The early version was a murder story.** Through 1891 the hero killed the merchant's daughter with an axe and fled to drown himself; the 1898 reworking cut the killing, turning a crime-and-suicide ending into a collapse from which recovery is still possible.
- **Nicholas I appears in the text as himself** — the fiancée's past affair with the Tsar is the plot's hinge — and the imperial censor cut that material from the 1911 Russian edition, restored only in the Berlin edition the next year.

The verdict on the dive's central question — that the work is about pride, not lust — is *confirms and extends*. Unusually, the mainstream already reads it this way; the dive's contribution is to ground that reading in Tolstoy's own letters from the writing years, which the criticism rarely cites.
