*A Confession* is where the novelist becomes the preacher — the book in which Tolstoy, at the height of his fame, tells how he lost any answer to "why live?", came close to suicide, and found a way to go on living in the faith of the labouring people. It is filed as the record of his "conversion." Two things the corpus shows complicate that filing — and a third unsettles even the title.

**It was not written in a rush, and Tolstoy's own word was not "conversion."** The finished surface reads like a single crisis seized whole, but the Jubilee editors trace the work back to 1877, and a complete, untitled treatise of 1879 was reworked until *A Confession* grew out of its first chapter; it stood finished by about mid-1881 and was proof-corrected in 1882. The genesis is visible in the letters: a year before any sustained drafting he had already put the question to Strakhov — «что я такое?» — and through the autumn of 1879 he names the work only obliquely and guards it: *"work for myself, which I will never print."* This project's [crisis dive](../../../../docs/research/themes/crisis/index.html) found that his own term for the change was «переворот» — an *overturning*, not the religious-technical "conversion"; his own later verdict, in an 1892 letter to Georges Dumas, is that the change "did not happen all at once" but lay "in embryo" in his earlier work.

**It was suppressed before it could appear.** Set in type for *Русская мысль* in May 1882, it was cut from the issue by the ecclesiastical censorship and the sheets sent for destruction; the journal printing never reached the public. It spread instead in hectographed copies sold at three rubles, was first published abroad (Geneva, M. Elpidin, 1884), reissued by Chertkov's émigré press in 1901, and was not legally printed in Russia until 1906. The Ober-Procurator Pobedonostsev stood behind the suppression — but the 1901 Synod edict that declared Tolstoy fallen from the Church, read in full, names *no individual works*: tying *A Confession* to the excommunication overstates the record.

**The title isn't his.** The manuscripts and proofs carry working titles — «Вступление к ненапечатанному сочинению», «Что я?», «Как я потерял смысл жизни и в чем нашел его». «Исповедь» first appears in print in Elpidin's 1884 edition; Tolstoy accepted it only by 1885. The English tradition inherited the same instability — "How I Came to Believe" (1885), then "My Confession," then Maude's standard "A Confession."

The full dive — sixteen-chapter structural read of the text, the composition-window letters and diaries, the Moscow circle (Fedorov, the peasant Sutaev, Alekseev) who first read his gospel synthesis, the publication and translation history, a heavy visual sweep, and the scholarship triangulated against the primary record — is here: [A Confession (Исповедь)](../../../../docs/research/works/non-fiction/personal-papers/1879-1882-a-confession/index.html). Every Russian quotation is checked byte-for-byte against its source (27/27). It also carries a proposal to fill the work's near-empty record — beginning with the two fields that are currently wrong: *A Confession* **was** published in Tolstoy's lifetime (Geneva 1884; Russia 1906).
