The third and last of three corpus dives into the labels attached to Tolstoy's late religion — after *Tolstoyism* and *Christian anarchism* — takes the plainest label of all: **Christian**.

The bare word is everywhere in the late writings, so a keyword search answers nothing. The checkable question is the *responsive* one: when someone asked Tolstoy point-blank whether he was a Christian, what did he reply? The dive reads the replies in his correspondence, the diary, and the 1901 answer to the Holy Synod.

The answer is neither yes nor no but a redefinition. Asked directly, he tended to decline the word in its ordinary, church sense — to one correspondent in 1903, flatly: "I am not a Christian in the generally accepted sense of the word." He argued one cannot really call oneself a Christian at all: unlike being a Jew, a Muslim or a churchman — identities fixed by an outward act — Christianity is marked by no act, so the word names a direction one strives toward, not a status one holds. He was indifferent to the name itself, and held that true Christians do not go about proclaiming themselves Christians.

Where he kept a positive term in his own voice it was the verbal one — "a follower of Christ" — and at the furthest reach, in 1910, he dissolved every label at once: "not a Christian, not a Russian, not a nobleman… but a worker of God, sent into the world."

Yet he did not abandon Christianity. He kept and redefined "true Christianity" — for him the perennial truth shared by all the great religious teachings — said plainly "I try to be a Christian," and would even grant the name, by deed, to a man who refused it. Against this stood "church Christianity," which weeks before his excommunication he called "the greatest enemy of Christ." The public capstone is the reply to the Synod: renouncing the Church, he framed the break itself as fidelity, and closed by professing "this Christianity, as I understand it."

So the hunch that the church-coding of the word would push him to reframe rather than claim it holds — with the important qualification that he reframes rather than simply rejects: he strips the word of its institutional meaning and keeps only what is left.
