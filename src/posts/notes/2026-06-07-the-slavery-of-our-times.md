The day after Christmas 1899, Tolstoy went to the Moscow–Kazan railway goods station to check something he could not believe. A weigher he knew there — Afanasy Ageev, a former Yasnaya Polyana servant — had told him the freight loaders worked thirty-six hours at a stretch. He saw it was true, and began writing the next day. The four-page note he started, «Самый дешевый товар» (The Cheapest Commodity), grew over 1900 into *The Slavery of Our Times* (*Рабство нашего времени*).

This corpus dive reads the treatise from its own text (PSS Tom 34) and reconstructs how it was written from the 1900 diary and letters. The argument is a single descending chain. Modern wage labour, Tolstoy contends, is itself a form of slavery — harder, he says, than the work "no cruellest slaveholder would have sent his slaves" to. It is not abolished but only ever re-formed: "one means of enslavement is abolished only when another has already replaced it." Its causes are three — landlessness, taxes, and the manufactured needs of luxury, the last the firmest; its supports are three laws — on land, taxes, and property; and a law is nothing but "a rule established by people who command organized violence." So the chain runs from the loaders' misery down to its root: slavery rests on laws, laws on violence, and "organized violence is government." The remedy is therefore not a better government or a new economy but the withdrawal of consent — refuse to serve, to pay voluntary taxes, to call on the state to guard your property.

It is the work in which Tolstoy is closest to the socialists, the Georgists and Marx, and most sharply divided from them: he shares their diagnosis and rejects every remedy as a fresh form of the disease. He quotes Marx's *Capital* on the bloody laws that drove the peasantry into wage discipline — he had read and annotated it — and then turns that history against the Marxist solution. He credits Henry George's land reform and rejects George's single tax in the same breath, the one place (scholars note) where he "comes out against George's economics." The late drafts he made, in his own words to his daughter, "more and more venomous."

Banned in Russia, it was published first by Chertkov's Free Word press in England in 1900 and translated by Aylmer Maude the same year; at home a newspaper that set the first chapters was threatened with closure, and the full Russian text waited until 1917.
