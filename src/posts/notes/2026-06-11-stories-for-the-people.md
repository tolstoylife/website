New corpus dive: [Stories for the People (народные рассказы)](https://github.com/tolstoylife/tolstoy.life/tree/main/docs/research/themes/stories-for-the-people) — a theme-dive across Tolstoy's folk tales of 1881–1887 and the Posrednik cheap-editions machinery that carried them.

The primary record runs from the bylina-singer Shchegolyonok's 1879 visit (the oral source of three tales) through the operational letters to Chertkov — the «для народа» filter, the 1½-kopeck pricing, the instruction to Sytin to sell «как можно дешевле» — to the censors' verdicts (both «Чем люди живы» and «Иван-дурак» arrested in February 1887) and Tolstoy's late self-judgments.

Two findings stand out. The 1883 diary records why the cycle took the parable form: a conventional novel of peasant life was structurally impossible — «Что за нелепость!» — so the moral parable replaced it. And the self-verdict never settled: the famous *What Is Art?* footnote (1898) silently consigns the tales to Tolstoy's own «дурное искусство», yet on 24 October 1910, two weeks before his death, he graded the Posrednik leaflets in four sorts and put nine of the tales in the top grade — with Pushkin's «Станционный смотритель» in the fourth.

With the two drama dives this closes an arc: *The Power of Darkness* was written for the people and kept from them; *The Fruits of Enlightenment* was written for the gentry; the folk tales were written for the people and reached them — in millions of kopeck booklets.

The dossier carries six work-record proposals (What Men Live By, Where Love Is God Is, Two Old Men, How Much Land Does a Man Need?, Ivan the Fool, The Three Hermits) and a 23-entity routing map for later wiki ingestion.
