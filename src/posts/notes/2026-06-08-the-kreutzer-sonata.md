Tolstoy's most scandalous fiction is also his most misread. The standard frame — a misogynist's veiled attack on his own marriage — treats the novella as grievance and its Afterword as an embarrassing appendix. The primary record reads differently: a doubting, nine-times-rewritten attempt to put a Gospel argument about sexuality into a murderer's mouth, written across two years (1887 → late 1890), followed by a separate prose text in which Tolstoy steps out from behind the murderer to say which parts are his. This dive reads the two together — against the composition diaries (Tom 50–51), the letters (Tom 64–65), and the editors' «История писания» — and treats the Afterword as a first-class text with its own record, mirroring the PSS, which catalogues it as a distinct work.

The marquee question — *how far does Tolstoy endorse Pozdnyshev?* — is answerable from the record, and the answer is precise: **Tolstoy owns the doctrine, but not its modality.** In the Afterword he calls the conclusions «своим выводам» — my own conclusions, reached against his own resistance («Я ужасался своим выводам»); to an English correspondent who asked whether he really meant total abstinence he wrote «Отвечаю: да» (I answer: yes). But the same Afterword reframes chastity as «идеал… а не правило» — an asymptotic ideal, a compass-bearing, not the despairing rule the murderer voices in the novella. The flat "Pozdnyshev = Tolstoy" reading and the pure ironic-distance reading are both half-right: the argument is Tolstoy's; the rule, and the knife, are Pozdnyshev's.

The censorship fight is itself the reception. Banned from independent print on Pobedonostsev's and Feoktistov's authority (Dec 1889), the work swept Russia underground in hundreds of lithographed copies — the eighth redaction, sharper than the printed ninth (the cut «случные жеребцы» stud-horse passage, the softened «обман и насилие»). It reached legal print in Russia only inside Collected Works vol. 13 (June 1891), and only after Sofia Tolstaya obtained a personal audience with Alexander III. Tolstoy reported the permission flatly, "at which I am not at all glad."
