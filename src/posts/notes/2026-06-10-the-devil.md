Tolstoy drafted *The Devil* (Дьявол) in two weeks of November 1889 — in the same fortnight he was finishing *The Kreutzer Sonata* — then hid it for almost twenty years, gave it a second ending and its title around 1909, and never printed it. It came out only after his death, in 1911. A landowner, Evgeny Irtenev, is destroyed by an involuntary sexual obsession with a peasant woman, Stepanida; the story exists in two endings, one in which he shoots himself and one in which he shoots her.

The title points at the woman. The dive tested whether the work does too — and found it doesn't.

A few things the primary record makes plain:

- **The "devil" is Evgeny's projection, not the author's verdict.** In the murder ending, the exact sentence in which he calls Stepanida the devil is the sentence in which he denies God and kills her: «Да нет никакого Бога. Есть дьявол. И это она. Он овладел мной.» The epigraph (Matthew 5:28–30 — pluck out *your own* offending eye) and the suicide ending (he turns the gun on himself) both put the destroying force inside the man.
- **The two endings aren't indecision.** Tolstoy worked the structure out in his diary on 18 November 1889 — "two lives… two exits… and finally a third: to kill himself." Suicide blames the man; murder blames the woman. The work refuses to choose.
- **It's his most autobiographical fiction.** It's built on his own 1858–60 affair with the married peasant Aksinya Bazykina, whose alternation of attraction and revulsion in his diaries is the exact mechanism he gives Evgeny. He told his biographer there was "a hint" of it in the story.
- **He suppressed it himself.** In 1898 he sold *Resurrection* and *Father Sergius* to fund the Doukhobor emigration — and withdrew *The Devil* in his own hand, because "the motif is the same as in *Father Sergius*." His wife found the manuscript in 1909, and it reopened an old wound.

Against the mainstream reading of Stepanida as a peasant *femme fatale*, the marquee verdict is **contradicts + extends**: the corpus locates the "devil" in the man's own will, and supplies the grounding that scholarship — where it engages the novella at all — has not.
