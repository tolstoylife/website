New corpus dive: [The late voice — encryption & compression (1900–1910)](https://github.com/tolstoylife/tolstoy.life/tree/main/docs/research/late-voice-encryption-compression) — a theme-dive across the whole late period, following one phenomenon through every genre and channel: how the late Tolstoy said what he could not legally say at home. It grew out of the 1903 folk-tales dive's keystone — «этого-то я не могу сказать в русском легальном издании» — chased across the corpus as a phenomenon, not a single cluster.

Two threads. The first is **encryption under censorship.** A truth that could not pass the legal Russian censor could still be told if it changed either its *genre* — a legend or parable says what a treatise would have had banned — or its *channel*: abroad through Chertkov's «Свободное слово» press in England, or posthumously, or withheld entirely. The dive maps an eight-tier channel spectrum from legal-uncensored down to author-withheld, and finds the rule stated plainly to the man who ran the route: in 1900 Tolstoy commits that everything going abroad must pass «только через вас». The sharpest single exhibit is *Resurrection*, whose prison-Eucharist survived the legal «Нива» serial as four words — «Началось богослужение.» — while the complete text was printed at the same moment at Chertkov's Purleigh press.

The second thread is **compression** — the same impulse turned on the doctrine itself. The late metaphysics contracts to a formula and finally to the four wisdom-anthologies: Мысли мудрых людей (1903) → Круг чтения (1906) → На каждый день (1909) → Путь жизни (1910), one project squeezed four times. Across the series the attributions drop away (by 1910 Tolstoy «нахожу неудобным подписывать их именами их авторов»), the calendar is abandoned for thematic chapters, and whole paragraphs carry verbatim from book to book. The two threads fuse: the daily-anthology form let a church-and-state critique sit as one unsigned maxim among foreign sages — and the publisher still cut some entries for the censor.

English scholarship confirms the abroad channel (Alston 2014; Holman 1988) and the *Resurrection* mutilation, but does not reach the dive's three live findings — that the folk-tale form was chosen as a deliberate answer to censorship, that the abroad route ran on an explicit protocol, and that the anthologies' compression also lowered their censorship profile. The contested "Chertkov the controlling false disciple" frame (Popoff 2014) is read critically: the primary record shows Tolstoy stating the channel as his own settled wish, freely and repeatedly, for a decade.

The dossier carries 57 byte-verified citations (56/56 pass; one fusion row points at the publisher's censored-out-thoughts file), an eight-entity routing map for later wiki ingestion (Chertkov and the «Свободное слово» press first), and four anthology work-record creation proposals (genre `anthology`) with a flagged shelving question. A light visuals sweep found everything but a «Свободное слово» masthead.
