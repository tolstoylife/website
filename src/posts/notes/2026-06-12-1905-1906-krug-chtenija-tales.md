The third movement of Tolstoy's народный рассказ project — after the [1880s Posrednik cycle](/research/) and the 1903 Kishinev tales — is six original stories written in 1905–06 for the weekly-reading (недельное чтение) slot of his anthology *Круг чтения*: «Алёша Горшок», «Корней Васильев», «Ягоды», «Молитва», «За что?» and «Божеское и человеческое».

Two things stand out from the primary record. First, the form changed: where the 1903 tales were legend and parable, five of these six are realist fiction — a character study drawn from a real Yasnaya Polyana servant, a domestic tragedy across forty years, a social-contrast tale of the 1905 gentry, historical fiction about a Polish exile, and a realist tale of an executed revolutionary. Only «Молитва» keeps the older dream-vision shape.

Second, the politics is not incidental. Tolstoy said he built *Круг чтения* with «именно нецензурное» in mind — to keep what the censor would cut — and two of the tales do exactly that: «За что?» names and condemns Nicholas I and shows the state's gauntlet, and «Божеское и человеческое» indicts capital punishment and the state-church, both written amid the 1905 revolution and its executions. As fiction in an anthology they carried what a signed treatise could not legally print. The strategy half-worked: a year after Tolstoy's death the anthology's editor, Gorbunov-Posadov, was tried and sentenced to a year in a fortress for publishing it.

These six are also the slot English readers never met — the only English *Круг чтения* drops all 52 weekly stories, and five of the six had no English translation until 2000.
