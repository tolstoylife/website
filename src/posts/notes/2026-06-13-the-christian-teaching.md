For two and a half years Tolstoy called it his «катехизис». He began *The Christian Teaching* in the spring of 1894 in question-and-answer form, meaning to write the whole of his faith so plainly that — his own test — it would be "comprehensible to Vanichka and the yard-keeper." It grew into something else: 2154 manuscript leaves, a structure he tore up and rebuilt, and a book he finished only in rough and never to his own satisfaction. Chertkov published it abroad in 1898 with a preface warning readers it was unfinished.

The new dive reads the work against the book everyone knows for Tolstoy's late religion, *The Kingdom of God Is Within You*. The question was whether the calmer book is the milder book. It isn't. *The Christian Teaching* **systematises** the same doctrine — God known within as love, life as the birth of the spiritual self out of the animal, a three-part anatomy of what obstructs it (sins, seductions, deceptions of faith), and the path of liberation, struggle and prayer — and it **calms** the tone, ending on consolation rather than indictment. But the content does not soften: the critique of the state and the church is as radical as ever. What Tolstoy feared while writing was not that the book was too radical but that it was too abstract — and by his own 1896 verdict it failed his clarity test even as it said "everything I know, coherently and consecutively."
