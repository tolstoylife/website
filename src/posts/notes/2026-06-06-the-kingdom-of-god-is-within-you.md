*The Kingdom of God Is Within You* (Царство Божие внутри вас) did not begin as a treatise. In July 1890 Tolstoy sat down to write a short preface to a translation of two American non-resistance texts — Adin Ballou's *Catechism of Non-Resistance* and W. L. Garrison's 1838 *Declaration of Sentiments* — and the diary records the first day plainly: "Wrote a preface to Ballou's catechism." The preface would not stop growing. Within weeks he was telling Chertkov it had become "a large and very necessary article about the deception of the church"; within three years it was the 293-page synthesis that fixed Tolstoy, in the world's eyes, as the founder of a Christian, anti-statist pacifism.

This work-dive treats the book as a *work*: how and when it was written and who stood around it (the daughters Maria and Tatyana as copyists; Strakhov, who advised splitting the overgrown eighth chapter; Chertkov, who urged Tolstoy to soften the tone and was refused — "the tone expresses the feeling, and the feeling infects more than any arguments"); what it argues (Christianity not as a mystical teaching but as a new understanding of life; the state as organised violence whose army faces inward; the refusal to lie as the one free act); and the publication story that is itself a parable of the book's theme — banned at home, sent abroad by a relay of couriers, printed first in French in 1893 and in its original Russian only in Berlin in 1894, then flooding back into Russia in hectograph faster than the censors could chase it.

Its greatest afterlife is one reader: the young M. K. Gandhi, who read it in South Africa in 1894 and wrote that it "overwhelmed" him. The line runs from Garrison and Ballou, whom the book's first chapter resurrects, through Tolstoy to Gandhi — and on.

The dive is the **work** companion to the project's earlier thematic dive on [Christian anarchism](/research/) (the doctrine); it leans on Tolstoy's own diaries and the work's text, byte-checked, and attributes the genesis and publication facts to the Jubilee Edition's commentary.
