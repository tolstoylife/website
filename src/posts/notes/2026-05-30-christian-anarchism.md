Tolstoy is filed in the histories as a *Christian anarchist*. The label is useful, and it is not his. This dive — the second of three splitting an earlier combined survey — looks at the narrow, checkable question: when *anarchist* and *Christian anarchism* turn up in the corpus, what does Tolstoy's own voice do with them?

Three moments carry it, all from the prophet period:

- **1894 diary (Т.52).** Asked by "the Englishman" *how to be without government*, Tolstoy dissolves the question rather than answering it: the question is wrongly posed; the only real one is whether a person lends his conscience to a violent state or withdraws it. The anarchist answer, for him, is individual and religious — not a political programme. Six years before Eltzbacher made the question public.
- **1900 letter to Paul Eltzbacher (Т.72).** Eltzbacher's *Der Anarchismus* had just canonised Tolstoy as one of the seven principal anarchists. Tolstoy praises the book and then declines the seat: «я не анархист в смысле политического реформатора» — *I am not an anarchist in the sense of a political reformer* — and proves it from Eltzbacher's own index, which lists no page of Tolstoy under "violence," because Tolstoy wrote on non-resistance as religion, never on violence as politics.
- **1901 letter to Gabriel Sacy (Т.73).** The only place in all ninety volumes where Tolstoy uses «христианский анархизм» in his own voice — and even there third-person: *Babism* "has much in common with Christian anarchism." One step short of wearing the badge.

The phrase itself was a movement's before it was his: a term of art among his correspondents — Schmitt, Kenworthy, Davidson, Crosby, Ortt — through the 1890s, consolidated by Chertkov's 1905 booklet *O khristianskom anarkhizme*.
