The photographic companion to the [Tolstoy in art](/research/tolstoy-in-art/) dive. Where that one set photography aside, this one picks it up: the only colour photograph of Tolstoy (Prokudin-Gorsky, 1908), the Bulla jubilee portraits, Sofia Andreevna's family album, Chertkov's archive, the Tapsell photographs at Krekshino, and the heavily-photographed death at Astapovo.

The spine is Tolstoy's own letters and diaries — what he wrote about being photographed. A few things the corpus fixes in his hand:

- In **1856** he says the famous group photograph of the Sovremennik writers was made "at my proposal" — settling a small error in popular sources that credits the idea to Sofia (who was eleven at the time).
- The deepest note is not vanity or refusal but **indifference**: to Molochnikov in his jubilee year, 1908, he says he forgot to send his portrait card "because I cannot attach any importance to it."
- By **1909** the press camera had become a pursuit. Meeting a photographer and a film operator, his diary records that it makes him feel "not the divine but the vile Lev Nikolaevich" — while, the same autumn, he warmly approved the friendly camera of Chertkov and the English photographer Tapsell.
- The fight over his image was **old and mutual**: at the end of 1894, Sofia Andreevna had the negatives of Tolstoy photographed with the Tolstoyans destroyed — a rehearsal of the last-year struggle.

On rights: almost every photograph is public domain (the photographers all died long ago), but the Russian holding institutions charge for their own high-resolution scans regardless. The freest, highest-quality part of the whole record is, fittingly, the most painful — the Metropolitan Museum's CC0 album of the 1910 funeral.
