A primary-source dive on **The Great Sin** (Великий грех, 1905) — the essay where Tolstoy calls land monopoly «великий грех», the great sin at the root of the people's poverty, and endorses Henry George's single tax as the remedy. Written at Yasnaya Polyana from April to July 1905, during the Revolution.

The piece is two essays fused: an attack on the intelligentsia who claim to serve the people («Народные заступники»), and a Henry George essay. The dive pins the genesis from the 1905 diaries and the letters to Chertkov, including the day a peasant reproached Tolstoy to his face for preaching against land-owning while buying up land — «было и больно и хорошо».

Read in Tolstoy's own order, the essay is religious before it is economic. Private property in land is a sin, the same kind of sin as serfdom — and, like serfdom, it falls only when the people who profit from it wake to it. The cure he names is conscience, not legislation. Henry George's single tax enters only as a conditional concession — «при существующем государственном строе и обязательных податях», given a state and taxes that already exist, the least unjust way to hold land.

One fact the corpus can show where outside readers can only infer it: preparing the first proofs, Chertkov — Tolstoy's closest friend and editor — set one paragraph of the introduction apart and had it printed separately, recording his reason in a letter of 8 July 1905. The paragraph said the people's life is formed by inner moral activity, not by external forms imposed from outside; it stood, Chertkov wrote, against the rest of the essay, which insists the outward forms of land-use must change. So the two emphases were gently parted, with Tolstoy's assent. (Academic readers reframe this as a puzzle about Tolstoy's «anarchism»; the dive keeps to his own words and treats that label as the outside's, not his.)
