A primary-source dive on **The Great Sin** (Великий грех, 1905) — the essay where Tolstoy calls land monopoly «великий грех», the great sin at the root of the people's poverty, and endorses Henry George's single tax as the remedy. Written at Yasnaya Polyana from April to July 1905, during the Revolution.

The piece is two essays fused: an attack on the intelligentsia who claim to serve the people («Народные заступники»), and a Henry George essay. The dive pins the genesis from the 1905 diaries and the letters to Chertkov, including the day a peasant reproached Tolstoy to his face for preaching against land-owning while buying up land — «было и больно и хорошо».

The hard question is the one the prompt set: Tolstoy denied the state any right to coerce, yet here he backs the single tax — a state-administered fiscal reform. The dive's answer is **complicates, not contradicts**. He endorses the single tax only «при существующем государственном строе и обязательных податях» — given that a state and taxes exist, this is their most just form. And his editor Chertkov physically cut the pure anarchist sentence out of the introduction because it contradicted the reform argument, and printed it separately. The tension was managed by scissors, not resolved by argument. The specialist scholarship (Wenzer 1997) reaches the same verdict independently — a "concession" Tolstoy "compartmentalized" rather than settled.
