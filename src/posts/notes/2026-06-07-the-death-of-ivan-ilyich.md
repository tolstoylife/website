It is the most ordinary life, told as the most terrible — *"самая простая и обыкновенная и самая ужасная."* A successful judge furnishes a fine flat, falls from a stepladder hanging the curtains, and dies by inches of the bruise, discovering on the way that the whole pleasant, decorous existence he built was *"не то"* — not the right thing. *The Death of Ivan Ilyich* (Смерть Ивана Ильича) was the first major fiction Tolstoy wrote after his religious crisis, and reading it from the corpus turns up three things worth pulling out.

**It was filed as relief, not sermon — and Tolstoy said so.** The received frame reads the novella as the conversion's first fictional tract. The diaries cut against that: on 1 May 1884 Tolstoy calls it *"художественная"* work he needs as *"отдых от той работы"* — a *rest* from the treatise labour, the artistic kind. To Chertkov, a year before he finished, he insisted the piece related to his circle of followers *"только по форме … по содержанию ко всем"* — in form to our circle, in content, to everyone. That sentence anticipates the "universal art" he would theorise in *What Is Art?* twelve years later — in his own words, not the treatise's hindsight.

**The dates in the books are wrong by a year — and the corpus says so first.** Most accounts give the writing as 1885–86. The Jubilee-Edition apparatus rejects that: the literary work began in **April–May 1884** (the diary of 27 April 1884 is the first record), and Tolstoy's confidant N. N. Strakhov wrote the dating onto the galleys himself — *"Повесть (1884—1886)"* — the line printed in every lifetime edition. (A small corpus snag: the genesis diary entries are catalogued under "1883" in the digital edition, but the volume header and the editors both date them 1884; the filename is a cataloguing slip.) The hero, too, was real: Ivan Ilyich Mechnikov, a prosecutor of the Tula court who died of cancer in 1881 — brother of the Nobel-laureate scientist Élie Metchnikoff — whose deathbed reflections "on the fruitlessness of the life he had led" reached Tolstoy through his sister-in-law.

**The famous third-person tale began in the first person — and slipped the censor the treatises couldn't.** The manuscripts preserve three redactions: it started as a dead man's *diary*, handed by the widow to a colleague named Творогов who frames the story — *"Я узнал о смерти Ивана Ильича в суде"* — before Tolstoy threw out both first-person voices for the objective narration we know, then, on the galleys, stripped the Moscow place-names and brand names to universalise it. And where the religious works of these same years — *What I Believe*, *What Then Must We Do?* — were suppressed, the novella carrying the same conviction passed without censorship into S. A. Tolstaya's 1886 edition. The text that most escaped the censor is the one least reducible to a tract — which is also why it became the only prose fiction Heidegger cites in *Being and Time*, and a founding text of end-of-life medicine.
