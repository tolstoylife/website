Context: A primary-source dive into how Tolstoy and the people and places around him were depicted in fine art — oil portraits, drawings, prints and sculpture — from Kramskoy's 1873 portrait to Tolstoy's death in 1910. The question was practical: when was each work actually made, does a high-quality reproduction exist, and is it public domain or held under institutional control? The method was to read it against Tolstoy's own letters and diaries. Photography was left out by design.

---

A few things the corpus settled that gallery labels round off.

The first painted portrait, Kramskoy's of 1873, was a bargain Tolstoy half-resisted. He sat only because the painter came in person and threw in a cheaper second canvas for the family — "and my wife talked me into it." Two portraits were made at once: the one now in the Tretyakov Gallery and a family version still at Yasnaya Polyana. He spent the sittings, he told Strakhov, trying to convert Kramskoy "from the Petersburg to the Christian faith."

The artist the corpus documents most deeply is not Repin but Nikolai Ge — friend, portraitist, sculptor, and maker of religious canvases Tolstoy shaped by direct instruction ("repaint Christ," he wrote in 1892). Repin painted Tolstoy more than anyone — twelve portraits, twenty-five drawings, three busts — but Tolstoy was a sharp sitter, calling one Repin canvas "impossible — all made up" the same season he found Ge "very good." The richest single record is July 1891, when, in Tolstoy's words, Repin and the sculptor Ginzburg "sculpt and paint me" at the same time.

The paradox sits in his own treatise. The most-portrayed man of the century, writing *What Is Art?* against professional art, named Kramskoy and Ge — two of the men who painted him — as examples of the real thing.

On rights, the news is good and simple: every artist here died before 1955, so the works are public domain in Russia and the US. The obstacle is not copyright but the holding museums, which restrict and charge for their own photographs of these public-domain works. Some images are superb and free (Repin's "Tolstoy Barefoot" survives as a 2281×6490 scan); others — Yaroshenko, Serov, the Trubetskoy sculptures — are absent from open repositories and must be requested from the institutions that hold them.
